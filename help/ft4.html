OscarWatch has its own FT4 window for working linear satellites such as RS-44. It decodes the downlink, transmits your messages, keys the radio, moves the contact along for you, keeps your signal on frequency, and saves finished contacts to the logbook. You do not need WSJT-X running alongside it.

What FT4 is

FT4 is a digital mode for short, structured contacts. Your computer sends a tone pattern that carries a very short message, such as your callsign and grid square. Time is split into slots of 7.5 seconds. You transmit in one slot and listen in the next, so one back-and-forth takes 15 seconds. A complete contact (calls, signal reports, and a sign-off) usually takes about a minute.

On a linear satellite you transmit and receive at the same time (full duplex), so you can hear your own signal come back from the satellite. OscarWatch decodes that copy and shows it as an Echo. Your echo tells you that the satellite is hearing you, and how far off frequency you are, which OscarWatch then corrects for you.

Because every slot starts on an exact second, your PC clock must be accurate to within about half a second. Use Windows time synchronisation (or a tool such as Meinberg NTP) before a pass.
No internet in the field? If you have a GPS set up in Settings → Integrations → GPS, OscarWatch can time FT4 from the GPS instead. About ten seconds after the GPS has a fix, it compares the GPS time with your PC clock. If the PC is more than a quarter of a second out, FT4 uses GPS time for its slots, transmit timing, and logged QSO times. The Windows clock itself is not changed. See Clock source.

Before you start

  • Station details: set your callsign and 4 or 6 character grid square under Settings → Station. FT4 will not transmit without them.
  • Radio control: connect your radio under Settings so OscarWatch can apply Doppler correction. See Radio & rotator.
  • Audio: you need a way to get audio from the radio into the PC (receive) and from the PC into the radio (transmit). This is usually the radio's built-in USB soundcard, or an external interface.
  • Choose the satellite and mode: focus the satellite on the main window and pick FT4 in the Transponder mode list in the satellite panel.

FT4 is not available, and transmit is blocked, when:

  • the focused mode is FM (FT4 needs a linear transponder);
  • FO-29 is focused (its licence does not allow this mode);
  • AO-7 is focused (AMSAT has asked that FT4 is not used there);
  • your radio reports more than 30 W of RF power. OscarWatch reads the power setting on ICOM radios that report it over CI-V (for example the IC-9700) and on the Yaesu FT-991 / FT-991A. Turn the power down and try again. Radios that cannot report power are not checked.

Your first contact

  1. Open Tools → OscarWatch FT4. Only one FT4 window can be open; choosing the menu again brings it to the front. The waterfall starts scrolling and the status shows Listening.
  2. Click Settings in the FT4 window. Under Soundcards, choose the input (audio from the radio) and output (audio to the radio). Under PTT, choose how the radio is switched to transmit (see FT4 Settings).
  3. Press Tune. OscarWatch sends a steady tone. Adjust TX level in Settings until the radio's ALC meter barely moves and the power is where you want it (30 W or less). While Tune is on, OscarWatch also measures where the tone comes back and nudges your uplink so it lands on the red marker. Press Tune again to stop.
  4. Watch the decode list for a while. Stations calling CQ show as lines such as CQ EA1ABC IN63.
  5. To answer someone, click their line. OscarWatch builds your reply, picks the right slot, and enables transmit. To call CQ yourself, press CQ.
  6. OscarWatch then runs the contact for you: it sends your report, acknowledges theirs, and sends 73 at the end. Your own transmissions show as TX lines, and the copies heard back from the satellite as Echo lines.
  7. When both reports have been exchanged and the contact ends, it is saved to the OscarWatch Logbook automatically.

Press Halt Tx at any time to stop transmitting straight away. If nothing relevant is heard for 3 minutes, OscarWatch stops transmitting on its own so it never calls endlessly.

The FT4 window

Waterfall

The waterfall shows the receive audio from about 200 to 3000 Hz, with newer signals at the top. Each FT4 signal is a short bright stripe. The scale along the bottom is the audio frequency in Hz, which is the position of a signal within the radio's passband.

  • Green bracket: where OscarWatch listens hardest (RX). During a contact it follows the other station.
  • Red bracket: where you transmit (TX). Your echo should come back inside it.
  • Click the waterfall to move the green bracket to a signal. The red bracket moves as well unless Hold Tx Freq is ticked.

Decoding is not limited to the brackets. Everything within about 700 Hz either side of the green and red brackets is decoded, so you still see CQ calls near the green bracket while you transmit elsewhere.

Lines are shaded to catch your eye: messages sent to you, messages from the station you are working, callsigns you have not logged before, and grid squares you have not logged before. You can change these colours under Settings → Appearance in the FT4 window.

A trace on the waterfall only becomes an Echo line once the message is actually decoded. Echoes often arrive slightly late in the slot because of the round trip to the satellite; OscarWatch looks for them during your transmit slots as well as receive slots.

Status bar

  • RX / TX and progress bar: whether the current slot is a receive or transmit slot, and how far through the 7.5 seconds it is.
  • Slot clock: the current UTC time, time into the slot, and whether it is an even or odd slot.
  • Clock source (in brackets after the slot clock):
    • PC clock: FT4 is using the Windows clock. You have no GPS set up, or it has no fix yet.
    • PC clock, GPS agrees: your GPS confirms the PC clock is within a quarter of a second, so nothing needs correcting.
    • GPS time, PC clock +1.20 s out: the PC clock is wrong by the amount shown, so FT4 is timing its slots from the GPS instead. A plus sign means the PC is ahead; a minus sign means it is behind.
    GPS time usually runs a tenth of a second or so behind true UTC, because the GPS sends each time report slightly after the second it describes. FT4 copes with that easily. You can turn this off with Use GPS time for FT4 slot timing in Settings → Integrations → GPS.
  • Uplink / Downlink: the radio frequencies OscarWatch is setting for the focused satellite.
  • Status: what FT4 is doing now, for example Listening, Calling CQ, Answering, or Echo calibration.

Buttons

  • TX message: the text you will send next. You can type your own, but the buttons below fill it in for you.
  • Enable Tx: start sending the TX message in your next transmit slot.
  • Halt Tx: stop transmitting immediately (also stops Tune).
  • Tune: send a steady tone on your TX frequency to set the drive level and line up your echo. Press again, or Halt Tx, to stop.
  • CQ: call CQ with your callsign and grid. With Auto reply ticked, the first station that calls you is answered automatically.
  • Report and 73: send that message again to the station you are working, for example if they missed it. Both need a contact in progress.
  • Log QSO: save the current contact now, even if it has not finished. Normally you do not need this, as finished contacts are saved automatically.
  • Skip RRR: when ticked (the default), a contact goes straight to 73 instead of sending an extra RRR acknowledgement. This keeps contacts short during a pass.
  • Even TX slots: transmit in even slots rather than odd ones. When you answer a station, OscarWatch picks the opposite slot to theirs for you.
  • Hold Tx Freq: keep your transmit frequency where it is when you click a station (the default). Untick it if you want to transmit on the same audio frequency as the station you answer.
  • Auto reply: while calling CQ, answer the first station that calls you (the default). Untick it to keep calling CQ until you click the station you want. A contact you have already started still moves on to the report and 73 by itself.
  • TX audio (Hz): your transmit position within the passband (the red bracket).
  • Clear / Save activity: empty the decode list, or save it to a text file.
  • Settings: soundcards, PTT, modem options, echo calibration, and appearance.

Staying on frequency

Satellites move fast, so their frequency shifts all the time (Doppler shift). OscarWatch handles this for you in three ways.

Doppler correction

While FT4 is running, OscarWatch updates the radio's frequencies once at the start of each 7.5 second slot and then holds them steady, so the radio is not retuned in the middle of a message. The small drift within a slot is corrected in the audio itself. You can turn that audio correction off for transmit or receive in Settings → Modem, but it is best left on.

Echo calibration

Even with Doppler correction, your echo may not come back exactly on the red bracket (for example because the satellite's own frequency is slightly off). When OscarWatch decodes your echo, it measures the difference and adjusts your uplink so the next transmission lands on the red bracket. The adjustment is saved for each satellite and used again next time.

  • Corrections are made at most once per slot, so they do not overshoot.
  • If your echo is visible on the waterfall but not decoded, OscarWatch only makes small corrections (up to 50 Hz) from the waterfall, so another station nearby cannot pull your signal away.
  • Tune uses the same method on its steady tone. To avoid chasing another signal, it only corrects once two slots in a row agree, then checks the next slot. If the tone has not landed on the red marker, it puts the trim back, stops calibrating, and asks you to call CQ so your echo can be used instead. Calibration from the CQ echo is the most reliable method.
  • To see, change, or reset the saved adjustment, open Settings → Echo calibration. Use Clear for the focused satellite or Clear all for every satellite.
Leave the TX offset in the satellite panel at zero for FT4. Echo calibration adds its own adjustment on top, and two corrections pulling at once make it harder to settle.

Moving around the passband

In FT4 mode the radio's tuning knob does not change frequency. OscarWatch keeps the radio on the calculated frequency and resets it at the start of every slot, so turning the knob is undone within a few seconds. Move around like this instead:

  • Within the passband: click a signal on the waterfall, or change TX audio (Hz). Most FT4 activity can be reached this way without touching the radio.
  • To shift the whole downlink: in the satellite panel on the main window, set the RX/TX switch to RX and use the ±10, ±100, and ±1000 Hz buttons. The change takes effect at the next slot (within 7.5 seconds). Store Offset saves it for that satellite in FT4 mode only, so your SSB offsets are not affected.

FT4 Settings

Open Settings in the FT4 window.

Soundcards

  • Input soundcard: audio from the radio's receiver.
  • Output soundcard: audio to the radio's transmitter.
  • TX level: how loud the transmit audio is. Set it with Tune so the radio's ALC barely moves.

These are separate from the pass recording soundcard in the main Settings. While FT4 is running, pass recording is paused so that FT4 has the receive soundcard to itself; it can start again after you close FT4.

If you use a Virtual Audio Cable (or similar) to pass audio to other software, send FT4 transmit audio to CABLE Input and have the other application listen on CABLE Output. After changing soundcards in Windows, press Refresh and choose the device again.

PTT

PTT (push to talk) is how the radio is switched between receive and transmit.

  • VOX / audio: OscarWatch only plays the audio; the radio or interface switches to transmit when it hears it.
  • CAT: OscarWatch sends the transmit command over the radio control connection. This is the usual choice for radios with USB control, such as the IC-9700.
  • CAT port RTS/DTR: switches using a control line (RTS or DTR) on the radio's serial port. Use this if your interface was built that way.
  • Separate COM port: uses a control line on a different serial port, just for PTT. It must not be the port used by the radio, rotator, or GPS.
  • Manual (prompt): OscarWatch tells you when to key and unkey the radio yourself.

PTT lead and PTT tail add a short delay before audio starts and after it ends, for radios or amplifiers that need time to switch. Invert is for interfaces where the control line works the opposite way round.

Modem

  • Audio Doppler on TX / RX: correct the small drift within each slot (leave on).
  • Parallel TX echo decode: look for your echo in two ways at once during your transmit slots, so it appears sooner. It uses more processor time for a moment; turn it off only if the PC struggles.

Echo calibration

Shows the saved uplink adjustment for each satellite, in Hz. You can edit it, clear it, or clear all.

Appearance

Decode text size and the shading colours used in the decode list.

Logging

Finished contacts (ending with 73 or RR73, with both signal reports exchanged) are saved to the OscarWatch Logbook automatically. Use Log QSO to save a contact that did not finish. If the Logbook window is open, the contact appears straight away, and if that logbook has Cloudlog upload turned on, it is uploaded too. You can also add or edit contacts from Tools → OscarWatch Logbook.

Problems

  • No decodes at all: check the input soundcard, that the waterfall shows signals, and that the radio is in the right mode. If the DT column shows most stations well away from +0.5 s, your PC time needs syncing (or connect a GPS so FT4 can correct it for you).
  • The radio does not transmit: check the PTT method and output soundcard in Settings. With VOX, make sure the radio's VOX or data VOX is on.
  • Transmit is blocked: read the status line. It tells you if the satellite does not allow FT4, if RF power is above 30 W, or if your station callsign and grid are missing.
  • No echo: check that you are within the satellite's footprint, the uplink frequency is inside the transponder, and your power is enough. Use Tune to see whether your tone comes back on the waterfall.
  • Echo well off the red bracket: let a slot or two go by so echo calibration can correct it. If it keeps jumping, clear the satellite under Settings → Echo calibration and set the TX offset in the satellite panel to zero.
  • "Native FT4 library not found": see Advanced below.

More general help is on the Problems? page.

Advanced

How the Doppler handling works

While the FT4 modem is running, CAT Doppler is held for each 7.5 s slot and only stepped at the slot boundary, in the same way as OrbitDeck for iOS. Within the slot, residual drift is removed in the audio domain: the transmit audio is pre-compensated (with the sign reversed for inverting transponders), and the receive audio is de-Dopplered before decoding.

Decoder notes

The decoder looks for FT4 signals starting up to about 2.5 s into the slot, which covers most satellite echoes. When Parallel TX echo decode is on, a second pass that realigns late echoes runs alongside the normal decode during transmit slots. Transmit audio is prepared before each slot and starts on the slot boundary; the tone itself follows a half-second lead-in, which is part of FT4 timing.

Native library

Encode and decode use a bundled native library (oscarwatch_ft8) based on ft8_lib (MIT). Release packages include the right file for each platform:

  • Windows: oscarwatch_ft8.dll
  • macOS: oscarwatch_ft8.dylib (Apple Silicon osx-arm64 and Intel osx-x64)
  • Linux: oscarwatch_ft8.so

If the status line says the native library is missing, reinstall OscarWatch so the file sits next to the application or under runtimes/<rid>/native/. Developers can build it locally with CMake; see native/oscarwatch_ft8/README.md.
